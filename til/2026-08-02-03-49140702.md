# Today I Learned (TIL)

TIL about the revival of MkLinux, a port of the Linux kernel to the PowerPC architecture, specifically on an Apple Workgroup Server 9150. This news caught my attention as it highlights the feasibility of leveraging legacy hardware for modern software projects. The fact that MkLinux can run on such an old machine demonstrates the flexibility of the Linux kernel and its potential to be adapted to various architectures. This got me thinking about the implications of this for software developers: it shows that even with limited resources, we can repurpose old hardware and breathe new life into it, and also sparks interest in the possibilities of retro-fitting classic machines with modern operating systems and applications. This approach can be particularly useful for educational or research purposes, or even for creating functional, nostalgic systems that can be used for testing or development.
