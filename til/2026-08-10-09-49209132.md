**TIL: Tail-Call Optimization in Rust**
=====================================================

After reading Jimmy Ostler's in-depth article on tail-call interpreters in Rust, I learned that the language's ownership model and borrow checker can actually hinder the performance of recursive functions. Specifically, the lack of tail-call optimization (TCO) in Rust means that recursive calls are not optimized away, leading to inefficient stack usage and potential stack overflows. This limitation has significant implications for developers working with recursive algorithms, particularly those that require deep recursion. Understanding these implications will be crucial when designing and optimizing recursive functions in Rust, and I'll need to consider alternative approaches, such as memoization or iterative solutions, to work around this limitation.
