# TIL: Tail-call optimization in C is relatively recent

I just read an article on LWN that highlights a significant aspect of C's evolution. It turns out that tail-call optimization, a technique that optimizes recursive function calls by reusing the current stack frame, was only recently implemented in GCC (version 5.0 and later) for C. This is notable because it means that many old C codebases, particularly those that utilize recursive algorithms, may not have taken advantage of this optimization in the past. This realization has implications for performance-critical code, where recursive functions might be reevaluated for potential optimization opportunities using more recent compilers or manual tail-call implementation techniques.
