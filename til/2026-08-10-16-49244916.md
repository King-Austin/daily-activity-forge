# Today I Learned

TIL that the concept of "Itadakimasu" in Japanese culture, where one says "itadakimasu" before eating to express gratitude to the food and the farmers who produced it, can be seen as analogous to the principle of "Don't Repeat Yourself" (DRY) in software development. This principle advises developers to avoid duplicating code and instead create reusable, modular components to reduce maintenance and improve scalability. Similarly, "itadakimasu" promotes a mindset of appreciation and interconnectedness, encouraging individuals to consider the broader context and dependencies that contribute to the food on their table. This cultural insight can inspire a similar approach in software development, where developers prioritize modularity, reuse, and awareness of the intricate relationships between components, ultimately leading to more maintainable, scalable, and efficient systems.
