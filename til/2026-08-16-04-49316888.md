# Today I Learned

TIL that the personal homepages of programming language creators can be a treasure trove of insights into their design decisions and historical context. For instance, the website of Alan Kay, the creator of Smalltalk, showcases his vision for the language's object-oriented paradigm, while Paul Graham's personal website highlights the influence of Lisp on his startup philosophies. This got me thinking about the importance of understanding the historical context and design goals behind the languages we work with. It's not just about learning the syntax or APIs, but also about grasping the underlying philosophies and trade-offs that shaped the language's development. This can help us make more informed decisions when choosing languages for our projects and even inform our own design decisions when creating new systems or libraries.
