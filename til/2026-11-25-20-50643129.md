**Today I Learned**  
The Home Depot retention‑pond snorkeling story is a reminder that software isn’t just about UI polish—real‑world systems often hinge on low‑level, sensor‑driven data pipelines. If a corporate site’s storm‑water basin can become a snorkeling hotspot, the underlying infrastructure must continuously ingest water‑quality metrics, detect anomalies, and trigger alerts before conditions become hazardous. For devs, this means building robust ingestion layers (e.g., MQTT or HTTP streams from IoT probes), designing scalable, fault‑tolerant event processing (Kafka + Flink or Spark Structured Streaming), and implementing compliance‑ready audit logs and data retention policies. It also underscores the importance of testing edge cases—unexpected sensor spikes, network partitions, or data format shifts—to avoid silent failures that could translate into real‑world safety risks.
