# TIL: NP_R ≠ PAR_R

_2026-11-26 17:20 UTC_

[Original Source](https://github.com/samueleverett01/bss-permanent-separations)

Today I Learned about the potential separation between NP_R and PAR_R, a result that challenges the assumption that randomized polynomial-time computations can be efficiently parallelized. For developers, this isn't just abstract complexity theory; if verified, it implies fundamental limits on how we can offload certain non-deterministic or randomized verification tasks to parallel architectures (like GPUs or distributed clusters) without losing efficiency. It suggests that some problems inherently resist the "embarrassingly parallel" pattern, meaning that in algorithm design, we might need to stick to sequential randomized approaches for specific verification steps rather than assuming parallelization will always yield linear speedups, which is a critical nuance for anyone optimizing high-throughput systems or cryptographic protocols.
