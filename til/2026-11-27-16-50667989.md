# TIL: False Computer-Checked Proofs

_2026-11-27 16:41 UTC_

[Original Source](https://cr.yp.to/proofs/falseccp.html)

TIL: Daniel Bernstein’s paper on "False Computer-Checked Proofs" is a sobering reality check for anyone assuming formal verification tools like Coq or Lean provide absolute guarantees. The core issue isn’t just about buggy provers, but the inherent risk of "unsoundness" creeping in through trusted axioms, library dependencies, or subtle logic errors in the kernel itself, meaning a certified proof can still be mathematically invalid if the underlying system is compromised. For developers relying on formal methods for critical infrastructure or safety-critical code, this highlights that verification is only as strong as the trust chain of your toolchain; treating a machine-checked proof as a silver bullet without auditing the prover’s soundness or considering independent cross-checking is a dangerous assumption that could lead to catastrophic failures despite passing all automated checks.
