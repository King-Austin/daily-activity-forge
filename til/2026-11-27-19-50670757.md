It’s a significant signal that Michael Nielsen pulled his *Neural Networks and Deep Learning* book, a foundational text known for its clear mathematical underpinnings, suggesting that the rapid evolution of LLMs and modern architectures has rendered some of its specific pedagogical approaches or technical assumptions obsolete or misleading. For developers, this reinforces the idea that static resources in this field have a short half-life; relying on a single canonical source for intuition can lead to blind spots regarding current best practices in regularization, optimization, or data scaling, so it’s worth auditing our mental models against recent papers and newer, more dynamic resources rather than clinging to beloved older texts as absolute truth.
