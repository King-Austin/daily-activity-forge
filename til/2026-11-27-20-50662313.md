# TIL: Citroën Hydraulic System

_2026-11-27 20:27 UTC_

[Original Source](https://www.citroenet.org.uk/miscellaneous/hydraulics/hydraulics-1.html)

It’s striking to see how early automotive engineering already solved the "distributed state" problem that modern software architects are still struggling with: Citroën’s hydropneumatic suspension uses a shared hydraulic fluid loop where every shock absorber, steering, and braking component acts as both a consumer and a regulator of the same pressure medium. This isn’t just a mechanical curiosity; it’s a physical implementation of a decentralized, self-balancing system where no single point of failure exists for the entire vehicle’s dynamics, effectively creating a hardware-level consensus mechanism. For us devs, it’s a humbling reminder that "resilient, stateless, event-driven" architectures aren’t just trendy cloud patterns—they’re functional necessities that have been silently running in every car on the road for decades, proving that robustness often comes from coupling systems through a shared, fluid medium rather than rigid, isolated modules.
